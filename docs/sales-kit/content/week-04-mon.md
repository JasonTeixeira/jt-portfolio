My portfolio site runs 78 checks against itself on every deploy. The scorecard you see on the page isn't a number I typed in. It's the output of that suite. If a check fails, the site says so — publicly, on the page.

I built it that way on purpose, because of a thing that bugs me about how people in my field present themselves.

Everyone in QA claims to care about quality. The site is slow, the links are broken, the contrast fails, the claims are unverifiable — but the pitch says "detail-oriented, quality-obsessed." Words. I didn't want words. I wanted the artifact to be the proof.

So the portfolio tests itself the way I'd test a client's product. Accessibility, performance, broken links, the integrity of every claim on the page — 78 checks, run automatically, results shown. When something drifts, the scorecard drops in public before I get a chance to spin it. You've already seen one example of that: the day my own gate blocked a release over vulnerable dependencies and I left the failing run up.

The whole idea is that you shouldn't have to take my word for anything. A site about proving software works should be able to prove itself works. If it can't, why would you trust me with yours?

Everything's checkable at agency.sageideas.dev — including the parts that have failed.

## Why this works
The self-proving site is the single most differentiated asset Jason has; leading with the exact number (78) and the "the scorecard is generated, not typed" mechanic makes the claim unfakeable. Callbacks to the CVE story build narrative continuity across the series.
