Test automation

Playwright vs Cypress

Both are modern, open-source tools for testing what your users actually see in a browser, and both are a big upgrade over the old ways. Playwright is the broader tool: many browsers, many languages, one API. Cypress is the more opinionated one: JavaScript, a lovely developer experience, and a time-travelling debugger that people genuinely enjoy.

DimensionPlaywrightCypress
What it isA cross-browser automation framework (Microsoft)A JS-first, in-browser testing tool
LicenseOpen sourceOpen source (paid dashboard optional)
LanguagesJS/TS, Python, Java, .NETJavaScript / TypeScript
BrowsersChromium, Firefox, WebKit (Safari engine)Chromium family, Firefox, WebKit (newer)
WaitingAuto-waits on elements, very few flaky sleepsAuto-retries assertions, also solid
ParallelismBuilt in, freeBest via the paid dashboard
DebuggingTrace viewer, codegen, videoTime-travel runner people love
Best forBroad coverage, teams beyond JSFront-end teams who want great DX in JS
Pick Playwright if

You need to cover real Safari (WebKit), your team is not all JavaScript, or you want free parallelism and cross-browser coverage without paying for a dashboard. Playwright is the safer default for broad, cross-cutting end-to-end testing today.

Pick Cypress if

Your app and team are JavaScript through and through, and developer experience is what will actually get tests written. Cypress makes writing and debugging a test feel good, and a test suite that gets written beats a more capable one that does not.

The honest take

For a brand-new project in 2026, Playwright is the default I reach for: the multi-browser, multi-language reach and free parallelism win most of the time. Cypress is not a wrong answer though, especially for a JS front-end team that already loves it. The thing that reduces flakiness is not the logo on the tool, it is auto-waiting instead of hard sleeps and good test isolation, and both of these do that well.
