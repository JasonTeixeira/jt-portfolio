# The Content Flywheel — How the Channel Refills Itself

A backlog of 45 posts runs out. A **flywheel** doesn't. This is the system that turns the work you're already doing into a self-refilling stream of posts, so the channel never goes quiet and you never invent content out of thin air.

The rule underneath everything: **you are not a content creator. You are a QA engineer who publishes his work.** Every real unit of work — a mini-eval, a client question, a build, a bug — becomes 2–3 posts. If you're doing the work, you can never run dry.

---

## The core conversion: 1 unit of work → 2–3 posts

Every time you do real work, it splits into multiple posts. Here are the standing rules.

### Every mini-eval you run → 2 posts
The free mini-eval is the top of your offer ladder *and* your richest content source. Every one you run produces:
1. **A "failure mode I found this week" post** (anonymized — never name the company, never quote their data). *"I ran a free eval on a support chatbot this week. It passed 40 of 45 cases. The 5 it failed all had the same shape: [pattern]. Here's why that pattern breaks LLM features."* — This is a **proof story + educational** hybrid, and it's unfakeable because it happened.
2. **A how-to that generalizes the fix.** *"Here's how to catch the failure mode I described Monday, in your own eval, in 15 minutes."* — Routes to llm-eval-gate.

Anonymization rule: describe the *shape* of the failure (injection got through, a citation was fabricated, an empty input crashed the flow), never the client. The failure mode is the value; the logo is irrelevant and not yours to share.

### Every client question → 1 article/post
If a prospect or client asks it, other buyers are wondering it. The moment someone asks *"how do you even test something non-deterministic?"* or *"isn't LLM-as-judge just AI grading AI?"* — that question is a post. Answer it once, publicly, in your voice. You'll reuse the answer in DMs for the next year.

### Every build → 1 case-study-shaped post
Every time you ship something real (a new gate, a runner, an eval engine feature, a regression suite), it becomes a case-study-shaped post: *what was broken → what you built → what it now catches → the number.* The self-proving site, the /eval engine, the 85-runner QA OS all started as builds. New builds keep the proof-story pillar stocked.

### Every interesting bug or finding → 1 proof story
The false-lesson catch. The CVE that drifted in. The flaky 10%. Every genuinely interesting thing you find *is* a proof story — because finding it is literally the service you sell. When something makes you go "huh, that's a good catch," write it down that day. Those are the highest-converting posts you'll ever publish.

## Weekly production rhythm

The channel is ~40% posting, ~60% what you do around the posts. Structure the week so neither slips.

**Sunday — batch write (60–90 min).** Draft all three of next week's posts in one sitting. Batching beats daily writing: you stay in one voice, you don't context-switch, and a bad Tuesday can't skip a post. Pull from live work first (this week's mini-eval, the question a prospect asked), then from `BACKLOG.md` to fill the gaps. Schedule or stage all three.

**Mon / Wed / Fri — post (5 min) + first-hour engagement (30–45 min).** Post in the Tue–Thu 7:30–9:30am-ish window when you can. Then **reply to every comment in the first 60–90 minutes** — that window is the single biggest reach signal. Real sentences, follow-up questions, never "thanks!".

**Every day — comment on 5 buyer posts (20–30 min).** Find founders, eng leaders, and PMs talking about shipping AI features, and leave a *substantive* comment — a specific take or a caught nuance, never a compliment. This is non-negotiable and it's half the growth engine: it teaches the algorithm who your audience is, and it puts your name in front of exact-fit buyers before they ever see a post of yours. Five a day, every day, is the habit that compounds.

**Every day — answer DMs like a human.** The free mini-eval offer generates DMs. Reply fast, do the free look, write the failures up plainly. That diagnostic is the most important unpaid work you do — and per the loop above, it feeds next week's posts.

**Friday (10 min) — capture, don't create.** Note anything from the week worth writing: a failure mode, a good question, a finding. Drop one-liners into `BACKLOG.md` under the right pillar. This is how the backlog *grows faster than you spend it.*

## Repurposing — one artifact, four surfaces

When a post lands (outperforms your baseline), it isn't done — it's raw material. Push it outward:

1. **LinkedIn post** (the origin) →
2. **Field note on the site** — expand the post into a permanent, linkable page under your field notes / writing section. LinkedIn posts disappear down the feed; a field note is a durable SEO-able asset a prospect can find and forward.
3. **X thread** — break the post into 5–8 beats. Different audience, near-zero extra effort.
4. **A comment-ready snippet** — the sharpest 2 sentences, kept in a swipe file, to drop into relevant threads when you're doing your daily 5.

Rule: repurpose only what already proved it lands. Don't pre-manufacture four versions of an untested idea.

## The honest expectation — read this in week 6 when you want to quit

An owned organic channel is the **one path to $10k+/mo that survives adversarial scrutiny.** It is a compounding asset, not a slot machine. The verified expectation is **3 to 9 months of consistent publishing before reliable inbound.** Month one or two looks like almost nothing — single-digit likes, no DMs, silence. That is not failure. That is every account's baseline before the curve turns.

What kills people is quitting at week 6 because the numbers are small — right before compounding starts. Your proof is genuinely unfakeable; that's your durable edge. But it still needs reps and time to find the audience.

**Judge yourself on leading indicators, not lagging ones:**

| Leading (you control — track weekly) | Lagging (ignore until month 3+) |
|---|---|
| Posted 3× this week | Likes per post |
| Commented on 5 buyer posts daily | DMs received |
| Replied to every comment in hour one | Followers gained |
| Ran + wrote up every mini-eval | Booked calls |
| Backlog grew (captured ≥1 new idea) | Revenue |

The commitment that works: **3 posts a week, every week, plus daily engagement, for at least 6 months, without judging any single week's numbers.** Show up on the bad-numbers days. The flywheel makes showing up cheap — because the work you're already doing writes the posts for you. That's the whole game.
