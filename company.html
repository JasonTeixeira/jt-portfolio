Company overview · who we are, what we do, and the proof

We build AI features — and the proof they work.

Sage Ideas is a one-operator AI engineering and quality studio. For thirteen years my job was making sure software actually worked — the kind of QA where a missed bug costs real money. Now teams ship AI features on a demo and a prayer, and customers find the breakage first. So I build both halves: the AI feature, and the automated evidence that it does what you claim. 01

What we do

Two things, tightly coupled, because shipping AI without proof is how good products break in public:

  • Build the AI feature — LLM-backed assistants, RAG systems, agents, and the workflow automation around them, wired to real models and real data.
  • Prove it works — evaluation harnesses, test automation, and CI gates that make "it works" a measurable claim, not a vibe. Citation coverage, regression suites, eval scores that go red when they should.

The output is always the same shape: a feature that ships, the evidence it behaves, and a system your team can run after I'm gone — your repo, your CI, nothing retained after handoff.

How we engage

Every engagement is scoped and quoted in writing after a short call — fixed scope, never open-ended hours. There is no fixed price list, by deliberate choice: the work is priced to the problem, not to a package. The audit fee credits straight into the build if you continue.

01Audit~1 week · start here
A fixed-scope look at the AI feature you're unsure about: where it breaks, what "working" should mean, and what it would take to prove it. You leave with a plan whether or not we continue.
02Sprint~2 weeks · scoped
A tightly-scoped build or eval-harness sprint — enough to move one real thing from "we think it works" to "here's the evidence."
03Build~4–8 weeks · scoped
The full feature plus its proof: the AI, the eval/test harness, the CI gates, and documentation your team can operate.
04Operateongoing · optional
If you want it run rather than handed off — monitoring, regression coverage, and iteration on a retainer.
