The demo is the easy 80%. The 20% you skipped — proving it holds up under real inputs — is the entire reason people don't trust AI features.

I watch this pattern constantly. A team stands up an impressive LLM feature in a week. Chatbot, agent, RAG search, a generator. In the demo it's magic. Then it goes to real users and the story changes: it's confidently wrong sometimes, and nobody can say when or why or how often. So it gets walked back to "beta," or buried behind a disclaimer, or quietly turned off.

The feature wasn't the hard part. Modern tools made building it almost easy. The hard part is the thing everyone treats as optional: the evidence that it works across the messy long tail, and the gate that keeps it working as the prompt, the model, and the data all drift underneath you.

That's not a nice-to-have you add later. It's the difference between a demo and a product. A demo shows it can work once. A product proves it works every time, and tells you the moment it stops.

My whole bet is that this gap only widens. Building gets cheaper every month. Proving does not. So the scarce skill isn't making AI features — it's standing behind them.

Build the 80% in a week if you want. Just don't ship it until someone's proven the 20%.

## Why this works
A manifesto that reframes verification from "QA overhead" to "the actual product," which is the mindset shift that makes a buyer want Jason. No CTA keeps it conviction-forward; the demo-vs-product framing is sticky and quotable, and it sets up the offer without asking for anything.
