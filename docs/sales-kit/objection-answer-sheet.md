# Objection Answer Sheet

> **LIVING DOC.** Update this after every call. Write down the real objection, word for word, and what actually worked. The answers below are starting points, not scripture.

One page. Four objections. Say these in your own words, but keep the structure: acknowledge, answer with evidence, offer the next small step.

---

## 1. "We could build this in-house."

You could. In roughly three months of your engineers' time — time they'd spend learning eval design, gate wiring, and CI plumbing instead of shipping your product. I hand you a working, gated system in three weeks and train your team on it. When I leave, your engineers own it and know how to run it. In-house isn't the alternative to hiring me. It's what you get after hiring me, minus the three months.

## 2. "How do I know it works on OUR stuff?"

You already have the answer. The mini-eval I ran on your feature — before this call — is the proof. It found real behavior on your real product, not a demo. The pilot extends exactly that: same approach, your stack, your data, with pass/fail evidence you can open and read. You never have to take my word for anything. That's the point of the whole system.

## 3. "Too expensive right now."

Then don't buy the build. Start with the audit (from $750, credited toward any build). You get a written report on where your testing actually stands and what would break first. If you stop there, you still have the report. If you continue, the audit cost comes off the build. There's no version of this where you pay and get nothing.

## 4. "What about our data? Security?"

Fair question, and I'd worry if you didn't ask. NDA-ready — send yours or use mine. Access is scoped to the engagement: only the systems and repos the work requires, through credentials you issue and can revoke. Nothing is retained after handoff — code stays in your repos, and access ends when the engagement does. It's one page; I'll send the data-handling summary today.
